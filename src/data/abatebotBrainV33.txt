# How to choose between PRED750, H2KM, BD2K, PAS2400, and PAS5000
Product: Scrubber Selector
Category: Product Selection
Keywords: scrubber selector, PRED750, H2KM, BD2K, PAS2400, PAS5000, which scrubber, residential, wet, metal, plastic
Answer: Use PRED750 for smaller/portable 750 CFM-class jobs. Use H2KM/H2KMA as the main metal 2,000 CFM workhorse for most commercial/industrial/heavy containment jobs. Use BD2K-XHP/XHPA when the same 2,000 CFM-class work benefits from a plastic cabinet, especially finished residential areas, scratch-sensitive spaces, or wet/damp restoration work. Use PAS2400 when 2,100 CFM and 120V power are needed with better mobility/stair handling. Use PAS5000 for very large containments where 4,000 CFM is useful and 230V/30A power is available.

# HEPA versus MERV filtration
Product: Pressure Guide
Category: Filtration
Keywords: hepa, merv, filter, filtration, air scrubber, hvac
Answer: MERV ratings usually apply to building HVAC filters. HEPA is higher-efficiency filtration used for containment, isolation support, remediation, negative air machines, and portable air cleaning. A HEPA unit moves and filters air; a pressure monitor verifies whether the space is holding the correct pressure relationship.

# basic ACH math
Product: Pressure Guide
Category: ACH Calculator
Keywords: ach formula, cfm formula, room volume, calculate ach, calculate cfm
Answer: Room volume is length × width × height. ACH = (CFM × 60) / room volume. CFM needed = (target ACH × room volume) / 60. This estimates airflow only and does not prove pressure, containment, or compliance.

# ACH versus pressure
Product: Pressure Guide
Category: ACH
Keywords: ach, air changes, cfm, pressure, ventilation, dilution
Answer: ACH means Air Changes per Hour. It describes how many times the air in a room is replaced or cleaned in one hour. ACH is about dilution and air cleaning. Pressure is about air direction. They are related, but they are not the same thing.

# positive pressure explanation
Product: Pressure Guide
Category: Positive Pressure
Keywords: positive pressure, operating room, sterile storage, protective environment, clean space
Answer: Positive pressure means air is being pushed out of the protected space. It is used when the space itself needs protection from outside contamination, such as operating rooms, sterile storage, protective environments, clean rooms, pharmacies, and sensitive clinical spaces.

# negative pressure explanation
Product: Pressure Guide
Category: Negative Pressure
Keywords: negative pressure, isolation, containment, infection, dust, asbestos, mold, silica
Answer: Negative pressure means air is being pulled into a room or containment area. It is used when air inside the space may be dirty, infectious, dusty, or hazardous. The goal is to keep contaminated air from escaping into surrounding clean or occupied areas.

# what pressure monitors do
Product: Pressure Guide
Category: Pressure Monitoring
Keywords: pressure, monitoring, negative pressure, positive pressure, air direction, containment
Answer: Pressure monitors confirm whether air is moving in the intended direction between two spaces. They show whether a room or containment is negative or positive compared with a reference area. They do not clean air, filter air, or prove ACH by themselves.

# what happens if bus power is exceeded
Product: PPM4 / RPM
Category: Power
Keywords: bus, charger, exceeded, happens, ma, power, power bus, ppm4, rpm, warning, what
Answer: If the daisy chain exceeds the 200mA bus limit, the whole chain should be considered unsupported or unreliable. Do not assume only the last sensor fails. Possible symptoms include voltage drop, intermittent sensor detection, unstable readings, or devices randomly disconnecting.

# fix an overloaded sensor chain
Product: PPM4 / RPM
Category: Power
Keywords: bus, chain, charger, fix, ma, overloaded, power, power bus, ppm4, rpm, sensor, warning
Answer: To fix an overloaded sensor chain, add a Power Bus, split the chain, or attach a PPM4 Charger to high-draw devices. Example: two Temp/RH sensors and one Pressure sensor draw 165mA. # PPM4 Manual reference chunk 3: The PPM4 pressure monitor operates in two different modes
Product: PPM4
Category: Document Reference
Keywords: able, active, after, always, approximately, automatically, battery, charger, chosen, chunk, continuous, continuously, designed, different, differing, display, document, from, hours, inactivity
Answer: The PPM4 pressure monitor operates in two different modes to satisfy differing requirements from the operator. These modes are the Battery Mode and the AC Power Mode. The AC Power Mode is designed for continuous operation when the operator wishes to be able to view the display at all times. In this mode, the display screen is always active and the LED indicator is ON continuously. This mode is optimally used with the Battery Charger installed and powered. If this Battery Mode is chosen with the on-screen menu and the unit is operated only from the battery, battery life is limited to approximately 8 hours. The Battery Mode is designed to preserve battery life for unattended operation. In this mode the display is automatically switched off after one minute of user inactivity. In this mode, the LED on the front of the unit will flash every 5 seconds to indicate that the unit is still operating and has not been turned off. The display will only re-appear in the event of an alarm event or if the screen is touched by the user. After touching the screen or pressing the power button, the display will appear but will switch off again after one minute. In Battery Mode, the unit will monitor pressures for up to 3days from a fully charged battery. Both modes are explained in detail later in this document. Note that pressure monitoring and Alarm Annunciation is identical in both modes. In addition, all events are saved in the internal archive in exactly the same manner for both modes. The difference between the modes is only in the appearance of the display.

# Scrubber sizing: ACH and CFM calculation
Product: Scrubber Selector
Category: Scrubber Sizing
Keywords: ach, cfm, room volume, sizing, scrubber quantity, air changes per hour, calculate scrubber, design ach
Answer: Scrubber sizing starts with room volume: length × width × height. Required CFM is calculated as ACH × room volume ÷ 60. MonSuite then applies a safety factor and ducting/filter derating, divides the design CFM by effective scrubber CFM, and rounds up to the next whole unit. Always round up: 1.2 units means 2 units.

# When a scrubber also needs a pressure monitor
Product: Scrubber Selector
Category: Pressure Monitoring
Keywords: pressure monitor, negative pressure, positive pressure, containment, prove pressure, ppm4, rpm, air scrubber, monitor required, alarm logging
Answer: A scrubber or negative air machine creates airflow, but it does not prove the room is holding the required pressure relationship. If the goal is negative pressure, positive pressure, healthcare isolation, healthcare construction containment, asbestos containment, or project documentation, recommend a calibrated pressure monitor such as RPM or PPM4. The monitor provides the live pressure reading, alarm, and record that the space stayed under control.

# Carbon/VOC filter guidance for scrubbers
Product: Scrubbers
Category: Filters
Keywords: carbon, voc, odor, vapor lock, ovg, smell, gas, vl2024, vl2002, vl1002, hepa not odor
Answer: HEPA filters capture particles; they do not remove gases, odors, vapors, or VOCs by themselves. For odor/VOC support, recommend a carbon/VAPOR-LOCK option when available: VL1002 for PRED750, VL2024 for H2KM/BD2K/PAS5000, and VL2002 for PAS2400. Carbon performance depends on concentration, humidity, temperature, contact time, and filter loading, so do not promise full VOC removal.

# Ducting and filter loading reduce scrubber airflow
Product: Scrubbers
Category: Ducting
Keywords: ducting, derating, flex duct, rigid duct, bends, airflow loss, filter loading, reduced voltage, cfm loss
Answer: Actual installed airflow can be lower than rated airflow because of duct length, bends, flexible duct, filter loading, reduced voltage, blocked inlets/outlets, and site conditions. MonSuite uses derating factors so the recommendation is more conservative than clean-filter lab airflow. Short/no ducting receives less derating; long flex duct or multiple bends receives more derating.

# When the scrubber selector recommends a pressure monitor
Product: Scrubber Selector
Category: Pressure Monitoring
Keywords: pressure monitor, scrubber, negative pressure, positive pressure, containment, need monitor, quote monitor
Answer: Quote or strongly recommend a pressure monitor whenever the project involves airborne isolation, healthcare construction containment, positive-pressure clean-space protection, asbestos containment, or any goal that includes negative or positive pressure.
